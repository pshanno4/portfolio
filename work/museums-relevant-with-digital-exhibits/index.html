Designing Digital Museum Exhibits That Visitors Actually Remember

Project in 60 seconds

What it covers—and what Paul contributed.

Digital exhibits become memorable when they give visitors agency, connect software to the physical environment, and continue beyond a single screen. This Workinman article explains how game design, mobile extensions, hands-on interaction, shareable visual design, projection mapping, modular content, and collaboration can support that goal.

Writing for museum decision-makers

The audience includes curators, education teams, exhibit leaders, and organizations selecting interactive partners.

Grounded in studio examples

Real exhibit and interactive-media examples connect design principles to decisions museum teams can act on.

Collaboration as subject and process

The article argues that durable exhibits come from collaboration among curators, creative leads, designers, and technical teams.

Writing for museum decision-makers

The audience includes curators, education teams, exhibit leaders, and organizations selecting interactive partners. Paul translated design and development practices into outcomes those readers care about: learning, participation, accessibility, repeat visits, operational flexibility, and long-term relevance.

Grounded in studio examples

Examples involving ECHO, the North Carolina Zoo, the Strong Museum of Play, cooperative trivia, SketchTank, and other interactive formats show how the principles work across different spaces and audiences.

Collaboration as subject and process

The article argues that durable exhibits come from collaboration among curators, creative leads, designers, and technical teams. It was also produced through that kind of studio process, with project contributors and editors helping define the examples, scope, and claims Paul developed into the final copy.

Paul’s role

Paul organized Workinman’s exhibit experience and multidisciplinary input into the published client-facing article.
