Project in 60 seconds

What it covers—and what Paul contributed.

A board game can retain its rules and still lose its feel when it moves to a screen. This Workinman article treats digitization as a translation problem, not only a software task, and explains how pacing, agency, context, feedback, atmosphere, solo play, cross-format value, and live content preserve the experience.

Eight ideas, one argument

Paul structured the article so each recommendation advances the same claim: digital convenience should remove friction without removing the rituals, decisions, and feedback that make the physical game satisfying.

Eight ideas, one argument

Paul structured the article so each recommendation advances the same claim: digital convenience should remove friction without removing the rituals, decisions, and feedback that make the physical game satisfying. Examples from familiar adaptations keep abstract design principles concrete.

Research and tone

The piece required game-industry research and careful calibration. It had to be specific enough for publishers and designers while remaining accessible to prospective clients who may not use terms such as adaptive UI, reward feedback, or live-content cadence every day.

Studio process

Editors and studio contributors shaped the outline, examples, technical depth, and client-facing purpose. Paul converted that direction into an article with a consistent rhythm and clear transitions across eight distinct design concerns.

Paul’s role

Paul researched and wrote the eight-part article within Workinman’s collaborative editorial process.
