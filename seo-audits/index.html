Explore the judgment behind an audit

Which repair should come first?

The same website can need a different order of work depending on the immediate goal. Change the goal to see an illustrative repair sequence, then open each finding for its evidence and completion check.

Inquiry goal: repair a broken contact path first, then clarify the offer and align the search signals.

01 The service CTA leads to a missing page

Evidence to record: the source URL, link destination and confirmed response.

Repair: connect the CTA to the correct contact route while retaining the chosen service.

Completion check: follow the link on desktop and mobile; confirm the form opens with the right service selected.

02 The capability page leaves the buyer's questions unanswered

Evidence to record: the specific page and the missing explanation of applications, constraints or how work begins.

Repair: write the information a prospective client needs to assess the service, with approved project proof and a defined next step.

Completion check: confirm the page explains the offer, intended buyer, relevant evidence and information needed to request a quote.

03 The canonical, sitemap and final URL disagree

Evidence to record: the response chain, declared canonical and sitemap entry for the affected URL.

Repair: align those signals with the intended destination while preserving useful redirects.

Completion check: verify the final response, metadata and sitemap. Search Console is needed to inspect Google's selected canonical.

Illustrative findings and priorities, rather than an automated assessment of your website. A confirmed indexing block or outage can change the order.

The agreed review scope

Search foundations, content and the path to a useful inquiry.

Discovery and URL signals

Review crawl and index directives, canonical URLs, redirects, sitemap coverage, titles and internal links. Establish which destinations the site actually serves.

Content and evidence

Check the role of each page, overlap between pages, the questions it answers and the support behind important claims. Identify content repairs with specific examples.

Usability and inquiry flow

Follow the routes from reading to service to contact. Review relevant performance and accessibility-related usability issues, with practical checks for the proposed changes.

What the available access can establish

A public-site review can inspect served pages and observable behavior. Search Console, analytics, repository or hosting access may be needed for indexing decisions, historical performance, conversion data or configuration. We agree the necessary access and checks before starting.

Before we scope the work

Questions about scope, access and implementation.

What is the difference between an overview and a comprehensive audit?

A limited complimentary overview highlights selected public issues when offered. A comprehensive paid audit covers the agreed scope in depth and provides a prioritized report and implementation plan. An overview is not a complete examination of every issue.

Are the fixes included?

Audit delivery and implementation are separate unless the proposal explicitly includes both. I can scope copy repairs and implementation support, or provide a developer handoff with acceptance checks.

Do you need access to my accounts?

A public-site review can begin without account access. Indexing decisions, historical search performance, conversion data and hosting behavior require the appropriate data or access. We agree the needed access before work begins.

Can you guarantee rankings or AI search citations?

No. The audit can identify verifiable issues and useful opportunities. Search systems, competition and citation selection remain outside my control; measured business outcomes require implementation and an appropriate baseline.
